Ingenuity

A small helicopter that learned to fly on another world.

Abandoned but Not Forgotten: scroll to follow its story

A name from a student

Ingenuity was named by Vaneeza Rupani, a high-school student who suggested the name in a NASA naming contest. The name fit: flying on Mars is an engineering puzzle.

The air on Mars is only about 1% as thick as Earth’s, and gravity is about 38% as strong. To get any lift, the blades of this 1.8 kg (4 lb) helicopter had to spin at about 2,400 rpm, far faster than a helicopter on Earth.

Strapped under a rover

Ingenuity launched on July 30, 2020, from Cape Canaveral Air Force Station in Florida. It rode to Mars strapped beneath the Perseverance rover, folded up like cargo.

The pair landed on February 18, 2021, in Jezero Crater, a place scientists think once held a lake.

Alone on the ground

In early April 2021, Ingenuity was released onto the surface and Perseverance drove away. For the first time the helicopter was on its own.

It had to survive the Martian nights, which fall to about −90 °C (−130 °F), by using heaters powered by its battery and solar panel. If it failed that first test, it would never fly.

The Wright brothers moment

On April 19, 2021, Ingenuity made the first powered flight on another planet. It rose to about 3 m (10 ft), hovered for about 40 seconds, and landed safely on its four legs.

Under its solar panel it carried a tiny piece of fabric from the wing of the Wright brothers’ first airplane.

Five flights in thirty days

That was the whole plan: a technology demonstration with no more than five test flights in 30 days. Each flight pushed a little farther. On the fifth, Ingenuity did not come back. It flew one way to a new landing spot, proving it could operate away from where it started.

An aerial scout

Having beaten the plan, Ingenuity began a new job as an operations demonstration. It flew ahead of Perseverance and took photos from above, helping scientists and rover planners choose where to drive.

It crossed rough ground the rover could not easily cross, including a dune field called Séítah.

Looking at the debris

Engineers also used Ingenuity to learn more about debris left from Perseverance’s landing. From the air, its camera could see parts of the landing system that had landed on the surface, something the rover could not study from the ground.

Higher and faster

In its final phase Ingenuity became an engineering demonstration, flying experimental tests to find the limits of how a helicopter flies in thin air. It reached as high as 24 m (79 ft) and as fast as 10 m/s (about 22 mph).

The last flight

Ingenuity flew for the last time on January 18, 2024. Pictures taken afterward showed damage to at least one rotor blade, and its flying days were over. NASA declared the mission complete on January 25, 2024.

Why it still matters

Ingenuity was built to prove that flight on Mars was possible. Instead it flew 72 times and showed how aircraft can scout where rovers cannot easily go. Five years after its Wright brothers moment, it is still the first aircraft to fly on another world.
